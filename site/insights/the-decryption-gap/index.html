The Decryption Gap

Frontier models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google still read your data in plaintext while they work on it. Here is how far encrypted and confidential AI has come by late 2026, what the big platforms actually ship, and how an enterprise should choose.

The brief in one minute

  1. The gap. Every mainstream LLM service decrypts your prompt on the provider's servers to run the model. Contracts such as zero data retention limit what happens afterward. They do not stop the provider's systems from seeing the data.

  2. The practical fix. Hardware enclaves. Apple, Meta and Google run consumer AI inside attested confidential computing, and Apple now does it on Google Cloud. OpenAI has previewed the same idea for its API, and Anthropic has published a design and taken on a confidential-computing team.

  3. The cost. Enclaves are cheap when tuned. Studies on NVIDIA H100 and Blackwell GPUs measure 1 to 7 percent lost throughput, though a default stack can lose 13 to 27 percent.

  4. The frontier. Fully homomorphic encryption, where the server never decrypts at all, passed a milestone in 2026: Llama 3 with 8 billion parameters now generates a token in 18 seconds under encryption. That suits overnight batch work. It is far too slow for chat.

  5. The playbook. Climb a ladder: minimize what you send, isolate what you can, demand attestation where it exists, and reserve cryptography for narrow, high-value jobs. India's DPDP Rules already name encryption, masking and tokens as baseline safeguards.

01

Encryption has a blind spot

Enterprise data is usually encrypted twice, at rest on disk and in transit across the network. Neither layer covers the moment the data is used. To answer a prompt, a language model multiplies large arrays of numbers that represent your words. Those numbers sit as plaintext in an accelerator's memory, together with any documents retrieved for context and the answer the model writes.

If you host an open-weight model yourself, that accelerator is yours. Frontier models such as Claude, GPT and Gemini work differently. Their weights never leave the provider, so your data travels to them and is decrypted on their hardware. That is the decryption gap. The J.P. Morgan team behind EncryptedLLM describes the trade-off plainly: outsourcing inference "risks leaking the clients' LLM queries to the cloud provider."1

The exposure has measurable costs. IBM's 2025 breach study found that 13 percent of organizations reported breaches of AI models or applications, and 97 percent of those lacked proper AI access controls. Organizations with high levels of shadow AI paid about $670,000 more per breach than those with little or none.2 Models are also imperfect custodians of what they see. In the CONFAIDE benchmark, GPT-4 and ChatGPT revealed private information in contexts where people would not 39 percent and 57 percent of the time.3

02

Five ways to narrow the gap

No single control closes the gap. The options form a ladder. Each rung removes a party you have to trust and adds cost, so the right height depends on the data.

The first two rungs are available today from every major provider. Under Anthropic's zero data retention arrangement, the company "does not store customer prompts or responses at rest after the API response is returned."4 OpenAI makes the same promise to eligible API customers, and since September 2026 it has been rolling out Private Safety Processing, which runs abuse detection without staff access: "OpenAI personnel do not receive access to the customer content even when it is flagged."5 Both are policies about what happens after inference. The plaintext still exists while the model runs.

Minimization is the cheapest rung and the one most teams skip. A gateway that swaps names, account numbers and other identifiers for tokens before a prompt leaves your network, then swaps them back in the answer, removes most of what an attacker or an insider could misuse. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, list "encryption, obfuscation, masking or the use of virtual tokens mapped to that personal data" among the minimum safeguards a data fiduciary must apply.6

Isolation trades capability for control. OpenAI's open-weight gpt-oss-120b runs on a single 80 GB GPU under an Apache 2.0 license and comes close to o4-mini on core reasoning benchmarks.7 Google sells Gemini itself on Google Distributed Cloud, including fully air-gapped installations, generally available since August 2025.8 Neither option puts Claude or the newest GPT models inside your own walls.

What about differential privacy?

Differential privacy protects training data by limiting what a model can memorize about any single record. It does nothing for a prompt at inference time, and it costs accuracy: Google's VaultGemma, a 1-billion-parameter model trained from scratch with differential privacy, performs roughly like GPT-2 1.5B on academic benchmarks.9

03

Confidential inference is how the big platforms ship private AI

Confidential computing closes the gap with hardware. CPUs with Intel TDX or AMD SEV-SNP encrypt a virtual machine's memory, so the host operating system, the hypervisor and the cloud's administrators cannot read it. Since the H100, NVIDIA GPUs add a confidential mode that encrypts traffic between that virtual machine and the GPU. Remote attestation completes the design. Before a client sends anything, the enclave proves which code it runs, and the client releases its data only if that measurement matches a published one.

The consumer platforms adopted this first because their privacy promises are public. Apple's Private Cloud Compute, launched in June 2024, set the template: stateless processing, no privileged access for staff, and "verifiable transparency," which lets outside researchers inspect the software the servers run.11 In June 2026 Apple extended it to Google Cloud, on Intel TDX and NVIDIA confidential GPUs.14 Meta has run AI features for WhatsApp in confidential virtual machines under Private Processing since April 2025,12 and Google launched Private AI Compute for Gemini features in November 2025, built on its TPUs and Titanium Intelligence Enclaves.15 Microsoft offers a preview of confidential inferencing in Azure AI on H100 confidential virtual machines with Oblivious HTTP, starting with the Whisper speech model.13

The frontier labs are a step behind for enterprise customers. Anthropic published its design with Pattern Labs in June 2025. A small trusted loader runs in an isolated virtual machine, presents itself as a "virtual accelerator" and accepts only programs signed by Anthropic's build system, and keys are released only to servers that prove their state.10 Anthropic called the paper "a sketch of our research to start a conversation." In October 2026 the team of confidential AI startup Opaque Systems joined Anthropic in what reports describe as a license-and-hire deal. No product has been announced.16 OpenAI grouped its data controls under the name Private Intelligence at DevDay in September 2026 and previewed Private Inference, which coverage describes as confidential computing for model inference. Technical details had not been published at the time of writing.17

Sensitive data should remain encrypted except at the point where it's processed.

Anthropic, Confidential Inference via Trusted Virtual Machines, June 202510

Performance is no longer the obstacle. On H100 GPUs, overhead stayed below 7 percent for most typical LLM queries and approached zero for larger models and longer sequences.18 On Blackwell, a tuned stack lost 1 to 3 percent of throughput,19 and NVIDIA reports up to 98 percent of non-confidential performance on HGX B300.20 Configuration matters. With default settings, Phala Network's engineers measured a 13 to 27 percent throughput loss and a 34-fold slowdown in model loading, traced to the serialized encrypted bridge between the virtual machine and the GPU.21

Enclaves move trust. They do not remove it. In October 2025 researchers disclosed TEE.fail, a memory-bus interposer built for under $1,000 that extracted attestation keys from Intel's and AMD's confidential computing and undermined NVIDIA's GPU attestation. Intel and AMD consider physical attacks outside their threat models.22 Audits keep finding deployment gaps. NCC Group found a timing side channel in the relay that hides users' IP addresses in Google's system, which Google rated low risk,23 and Trail of Bits' 2026 audit of WhatsApp's private inference found implementation and deployment gaps, including replayable attestation reports, that Meta has since fixed.24 Verify attestation in your own code instead of taking the label on trust.

04

The cryptographic frontier: computing on data no one can read

Cryptography takes the hardware out of the trust equation. Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) lets a server compute directly on ciphertext. The client encrypts its prompt, the server runs the model on the encrypted numbers, and only the client's key can read the result. Craig Gentry proved it possible in 2009,25 and the CKKS scheme of 2017 made it practical for the approximate arithmetic neural networks use.26 Secure multiparty computation (MPC) takes a different route. It splits the data into shares across several servers that must not collude.

The hard part is the nonlinear math inside a transformer. FHE natively adds and multiplies, so softmax, GeLU and layer normalization must be replaced with polynomial approximations, and the ciphertext must be refreshed periodically by an expensive step called bootstrapping. EncryptedLLM, presented at ICML 2025 by J.P. Morgan researchers, moved that work onto GPUs. An encrypted GPT-2 forward pass fell from roughly four hours on a CPU to about 69 seconds per token on one NVIDIA A100, at 128-bit security and with benchmark accuracy almost unchanged.1 The authors are candid that "real-time chatbots under FHE remains out of reach," while summarization, drafting and fine-tuning on private data are now practical.

Then the curve bent again. In January 2026, CryptoLab and Seoul National University reported Sylph, which runs Llama-3-8B, a model about 65 times larger than GPT-2 small, on 128 encrypted input tokens: 20 seconds to read the input and 18 seconds per generated token on eight NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPUs. The previous best system needed 295 seconds on eight H100s for the same reading step. Sylph also handles a 4,096-token prompt whose last 128 tokens are private, at 22 seconds per token.27 For comparison, PUMA, the best-known MPC result, took about five minutes per token for LLaMA-7B in 2023.28

Three limits remain. Speed rules out chat and agent loops. The model owner has to run the encrypted model, so FHE helps only when a provider adopts it, and none of the frontier labs has announced FHE inference. The guarantees are also narrow: in EncryptedLLM's threat model the server learns nothing about the input, but nothing guarantees that the answer it returns is correct.1

06

Matching protection to your data

For most enterprises the decision starts with a data inventory. Classify what an AI system will touch, then apply the lowest rung that meets the risk. Spend more only where the data demands it.

Agents widen the gap

An agent does not send one prompt. It retrieves documents, calls tools, writes to memory and leaves traces, and each of those steps holds plaintext somewhere. Classify every data flow an agent creates and give each one the rung it needs.

07

What to watch in 2027

  1. 1. Enterprise confidential inference from the frontier labs

    Watch OpenAI's Private Inference preview and what Anthropic builds with the Opaque team. Look for independent keyservers and published measurements, which Anthropic has said it is exploring.10,16,17

  2. 2. Verification as a routine control

    Expect buyers to check attestation in their own code, to use open stacks such as OpenPCC, built on Intel TDX and H100 GPUs,30 and to ask for third-party audits.

  3. 3. FHE pilots on real workloads

    At 18 seconds per token for an 8B model, encrypted summarization and scoring fit overnight batches. The next milestone to watch is single-digit seconds per token.

  4. 4. Compliance clocks

    India's DPDP Rules bring their main obligations into force in May 2027,6 and Gartner predicts that by 2029 more than 75 percent of operations processed in untrusted infrastructure will be secured in use by confidential computing.31

08

The bottom line

In one paragraph

The decryption gap is narrowing from two directions. Hardware enclaves already let platforms promise that no one, including the operator, can read data during inference, for a few percent of throughput when the stack is tuned. Cryptography removes the need to trust hardware at all, and it is now fast enough for patient, high-value work. Until the frontier labs offer attested inference to enterprises, the strongest practical posture combines minimization, isolation and contracts, each chosen for the data it protects.
